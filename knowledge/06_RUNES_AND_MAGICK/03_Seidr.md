# Seidr

## In Brief

Seiðr is the ecstatic, shamanic spiritual practice of ancient Scandinavia. Originating with the Vanic goddess Freyja, it employs trance, rhythm, altered states of consciousness, and thread-working to perceive and alter the web of wyrd, communicate with spirits, and achieve prophetic vision (*spá*).

## Core Idea

Where Galdr is an active, vocal, conscious manipulation of sound and runes, **Seiðr** (pronounced *SAY-thur*) is a receptive, ecstatic, trance-based craft. In the *Ynglinga saga*, Snorri Sturluson records that it was Freyja who first introduced this Vanic art to Asgard, personally instructing Odin in its deep mysteries.

In historical Norse literature (such as the *Saga of Erik the Red* and *Völuspá*), seiðr is depicted as a communal or solitary visionary ritual centered around the **Völva** (wand-bearer or seeress). The classic ritual setup involved several distinct components:
1. **The High Seat (*Seiðhjallr*):** A raised wooden platform or chair upon which the practitioner sat above the ground and assembly, symbolizing ascension along the trunk of Yggdrasil into the liminal space between worlds.
2. **The Spirit Songs (*Varðlokkur*):** Chanted by a supporting choir of women around the high seat, these repetitive, haunting melodies created an acoustic carrier wave that summoned helpful protective spirits and guided the practitioner into deep trance.
3. **The Distaff and Staff (*Seiðstafr*):** Archaeological excavations (such as the Fyrkat grave in Denmark) have unearthed metal and wooden staffs held by völvas. The distaff used in spinning wool provided the primary physical and metaphysical metaphor: the seið-worker literally "spins," weaves, ties knots, and redirects the subtle energetic threads of wyrd.
4. **Prophecy and Vision (*Spá*):** In trance, the practitioner communes with ancestors, landvættir, and the Norns, answering questions about coming weather, harvests, sickness, missing persons, and upcoming community trials.

### Overcoming Historical Taboos: Ergi and Gender Sovereignty

In Viking Age patriarchal society, seiðr was surrounded by severe social taboos when practiced by men. A man who practiced seiðr was accused of **ergi** (unmanliness, effeminacy, passivity, or sexual receptivity), because entering a trance required surrendering rigid masculine control and allowing spirits to enter one's awareness. Even Odin was mocked by Loki in *Lokasenna* for practicing seiðr upon Samsey like a witch.

The Heathen Third Path directly confronts and **completely rejects** these historical patriarchal and homophobic prejudices:
- Surrendering ego-control in trance is not "weakness"; it requires immense courage, psychological resilience, and spiritual discipline.
- Odin himself deemed seiðr so vital that he gladly endured social mockery to master its power.
- In the Third Path, seiðr is recognized as a profound spiritual gift available to all sincere practitioners regardless of biological sex, gender identity, or sexual orientation.

## Key Principles

- **Receptive Trance:** Moving from the rigid, controlling ego into fluid, porous awareness capable of sensing the unseen currents of nature and spirit.
- **The Spinning of Wyrd:** Visualizing and working with the threads of destiny—loosening knots of trauma, binding hostile influences, and weaving threads of health.
- **The Wand as Center:** Grounding the practitioner's body while consciousness journeys across the branches of the World Tree.
- **Psychological Protection:** Maintaining strong personal sovereignty, healthy spiritual boundaries, and firm grounding back in Midgard after every working.

## Distinctions

- **Seiðr vs. Demonic Possession:** In seiðr, the practitioner maintains core sovereignty and intentional cooperation with spirits, rather than becoming a helpless victim of invasion.
- **Ecstatic Craft vs. Escapist Fantasy:** Authentic seiðr returns from trance with actionable, practical wisdom that can be applied to real life in Midgard.
- **Living Art vs. Historical Superstition:** Recognizing the psychological, energetic, and somatic realities underlying ancient trance techniques.
