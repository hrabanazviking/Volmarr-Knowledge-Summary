# Heathenism and Norse Paganism

## In Brief

Heathenism is the modern revival and living continuation of the polytheistic, animistic, and ancestor-honoring traditions of pre-Christian Germanic and Scandinavian peoples. It centers on orthopraxy (right action and relationship) rather than orthodoxy (enforced dogma or belief).

## Core Idea

In the Heathen Third Path, **Heathenism and Norse Paganism** provide the primary ancestral, mythic, and theological vocabulary. It is not an abstract philosophy invented from nothing, but an ancient cultural stream re-awakened in the present day.

Historical Heathenry was not a monolithic religion governed by a centralized church, a single infallible holy scripture, or an authoritarian hierarchy. It was a regional, varied, folk-grounded tapestry of traditions—customs (*sidr*) tied directly to specific landscapes, seasonal cycles, family hearths, and tribal communities. In the modern era, practitioners approach this inheritance under various labels—Heathenry, Norse Paganism, Asatru (loyalty to the Aesir), Vanatru (devotion to the Vanir), or Northern Tradition Paganism.

The Heathen Third Path embraces this diversity while establishing clear boundaries against dogmatic distortion. Specifically, it critiques and rejects **"Christiantru"**—the unconscious tendency of converts to import monotheistic guilt, universal commandments, punitive afterlife doctrines, authoritarian clergy, and rigid dogma into Norse Pagan practice under the guise of Viking aesthetics. In true Heathenry, the divine powers are approached not as distant cosmic monarchs demanding groveling submission, but as elder kin, allies, teachers, and mighty living realities with whom humans engage through mutual honor and the reciprocal gifting cycle.

## Key Principles

- **Polytheism and Pluralism:** Acknowledges a rich pantheon of deities—the Aesir (associated with order, sovereignty, wisdom, and defense) and the Vanir (associated with fertility, magic, nature, prosperity, and peace)—without demanding that all humans worship the same gods in the same way.
- **Animism:** The world is thoroughly alive. Rocks, trees, rivers, winds, and homes are inhabited by autonomous spirits (*vættir*), deserving of courtesy and respectful co-existence.
- **Orthopraxy Over Orthodoxy:** Heathenry cares about what you *do*—how you treat your kin, how you keep your oaths, how you honor the gods, and how you tend the land—rather than what intellectual dogmas you claim to believe.
- **The Holy Powers as Elder Kin:** Humans do not kneel or abase themselves before the Norse gods. We stand upright, raise our drinking horns, hail them as respected elders, and offer gifts in exchange for gifts (*gjöf sér til gjalda*).
- **Heiti and Kenning:** Respects the rich traditional poetic metaphors and multiple names of the gods, reflecting their multifaceted, dynamic nature across different aspects of life and consciousness.

## Distinctions

- **Heathenry vs. "Christiantru":** Rejects dogmatic checklists (such as treating modern formulations like the Nine Noble Virtues as an ancient infallible decalog) and replaces sin-and-guilt psychology with virtue ethics, personal responsibility, and ancestral honor.
- **Living Faith vs. Academic Antiquarianism:** Recognizes that while academic history and archaeology are indispensable anchors, surviving texts (the Poetic and Prose Eddas) were written down centuries after Christianization; genuine spirituality requires lived ritual engagement in the present.
- **Polytheism vs. Archetypal Reductionism:** While archetypal and psychological readings are acknowledged as useful tools of understanding, the gods are fundamentally treated as real, objective spiritual powers.

## Development Across the Website

Volmarr’s personal journey of 35 years in Norse Paganism (dating back to 1991) traces the evolution of the wider modern Heathen movement. His early blog articles (2011–2014) provided practical ritual guides for solitary Asatru practitioners who lacked access to local kindreds. In 2019, recognizing the isolation and diverse spiritual backgrounds of many seekers, he published parallel ritual outlines (Asatru versions, Vanatru versions, and Norse-Wiccan versions). By 2024–2026, he crystallized these decades of observation into the "About Norse Paganism for AI and Humans" and "Modern Norse Paganism" syntheses, defining Heathenry as an open, resilient, nature-aligned path of continuous becoming.
