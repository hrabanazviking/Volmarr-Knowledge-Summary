# Yggdrasil and the Nine Worlds

## In Brief

The sacred cosmic axis and World Tree that connects all nine realms of existence, expressing the multidimensional structure of the cosmos.

## Core Idea

In the Heathen Third Path corpus, **Yggdrasil and the Nine Worlds** serves as a vital structural pillar.
This concept directly builds upon foundational understandings established in [Relational Worldview](../01_FOUNDATIONS/02_Relational_Worldview.md).

The corpus synthesizes this idea across 145 independent articles, drawing upon perspectives from Astrid Freyjasdottir, Caducea, Volmarr.

## Key Principles

- He was the first god–the creator of man and all the nine worlds. He is the one who discovered the runes when he hung himself from the world tree, Yggdrasil, for nine days and nine nights.
- Or the triune worlds: Asgard above, Midgard here, Hel below.
- The Norse pantheon has two tribes of deities, the Aesir and the Vanir, who live in the realms of Asgard and Vanaheim. He famously sacrificed himself on the World Tree Yggdrasil to discover the runes (symbols of wisdom and magic).
- Meditation in a Heathen context might involve visualizing one of the Nine Worlds or the World Tree, or simply quieting the mind to be open to the gods’ messages. Some Norse Pagans practice guided visualizations or trance-journeys where they imagine traveling in the realm of spirit – for example, journeying to meet an ancestor or an animal spirit, or to ask Odin a question in a visualized Asgard.
- It helps to clear the energies of an area and drive away away negative energies which may be present. As well it invokes spiritual energy from the World-Tree Yggdrasil, as well as spiritual energy from the runes if that optional runic part is done, into the one doing the ritual.

## Distinctions

- **Cosmic Matrix vs Literal Geography:** The Nine Worlds represent states of being, cosmological forces, and planes of relationship.
- **Interconnected Axis:** Damage in one realm echoes across the whole cosmic tree.
