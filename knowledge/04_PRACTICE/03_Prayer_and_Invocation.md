# Prayer and Invocation

## In Brief

Prayer in the Heathen Third Path is an honorable, direct dialogue between sovereign beings and their divine allies. It rejects all forms of groveling, guilt, and self-abasement, standing upon the foundational posture of upright dignity: **"We do not kneel; we raise our horns and hail."**

## Core Idea

In monotheistic traditions, prayer is frequently structured around humility, confessions of unworthiness, pleading for mercy, and submission to an omnipotent ruler. For those coming to Norse Paganism from such backgrounds, learning to pray as a Heathen requires a profound psychological unlearning.

In the Northern worldview, humans are created with divine breath (*önd*) and conscious inspiration (*óðr*). The gods are not distant despots who take pleasure in our humiliation. They are elder kin, great chieftains, wise mentors, and cosmic architects who respect those who stand upright, speak the truth, and carry themselves with honor.

Heathen prayer takes two primary forms:
1. **Formal Skaldic Invocation:** Utilizing poetic structure, alliteration, traditional kennings, and established heiti (mythic titles) to honor the deities with beauty, linguistic craft, and reverence.
2. **Spontaneous Heartfelt Dialogue:** Conversing with a deity or ancestor naturally and directly—sharing hopes, expressing grief, seeking clarity, or speaking words of gratitude as one would with a beloved, trusted elder friend (*fulltrúi*).

The physical posture of Heathen prayer reflects this theology. The traditional stance is standing upright with feet firmly planted upon Midgard, chest open, head held high, and arms raised at an angle mirroring the sacred rune **Elhaz** ($\Psi$), the stave of divine connection, protection, and reaching toward the Holy Powers. Alternatively, one stands with both hands holding the drinking horn or offering vessel close to the heart before raising it in a toast.

## The Canonical Structure of Invocation

A traditional Heathen invocation follows a five-part rhetorical grammar:
1. **The Naming (*Nafn*):** Calling upon the deity by their primary name and sacred titles (e.g., *"Hail Freyja, Vanadís, Lady of Sessrúmnir, keeper of the Brísingamen!"*).
2. **The Mythic Precedent:** Recalling their legendary deeds and character, reminding them of the shared lore that binds us (e.g., *"As you taught the sacred mysteries of seiðr to the Aesir..."*).
3. **The Offering of Troth:** Presenting the gift that accompanies the prayer (e.g., *"Accept this cup of sweet mead, offered in honor and friendship"*).
4. **The Petition or Affirmation:** Asking for what is genuinely needed—not lottery winnings or selfish shortcuts, but inner qualities such as courage, clarity, endurance, protection, or peace (e.g., *"Grant me the steadfast courage to speak the truth without fear"*).
5. **The Hail and Closing:** Sealing the invocation with honor (e.g., *"Hail the Vanadís! Be welcome at our hearth, and departed in peace!"*).

## Key Principles

- **Dignity and Upright Posture:** Stand tall before the gods. Self-abasement insults the divine breath placed within you.
- **Petitioning for Strength, Not Dependency:** Heathens do not pray for the gods to do our work for us. We pray for the strength, wisdom, and luck to fight our own battles and build our own lives.
- **Poetic Craft:** The gods love poetry and eloquence. Taking the time to craft thoughtful, alliterative verses is itself a holy offering of *óðr*.
- **Listening is Half of Prayer:** Prayer is not a one-way monologue. After speaking, the practitioner remains silent, listening to the wind, the breath, the intuition, and the quiet movement of the wights.

## Distinctions

- **Dialogue vs. Groveling:** We address the Holy Powers as trusted allies and elder family members, never as tyrannical masters.
- **Partnership vs. Entitlement:** We do not command or demand favors from the gods like a petulant child; we honor them with gifts and respect their sovereign autonomy.
- **Grounded Reality vs. Spiritual Bypassing:** Prayer does not replace practical work in Midgard; it aligns the mind and spirit to perform that work effectively.
