# Local Computing and Personal Knowledge

## In Brief

Local computing and personal knowledge management form the technical fortress of the modern sovereign hearth. By running software, local large language models, and encrypted personal knowledge vaults exclusively on local bare-metal hardware, the practitioner wards their digital territory against corporate surveillance, vendor lock-in, and remote censorship.

## Core Idea

In the Heathen tradition, the homestead (*garðr*) was protected by physical boundaries, ancestral wards, and the conscious maintenance of hospitality laws. Outside the fence lay *Útgarðr*—the untamed, chaotic wilderness where external forces held sway. 

In the digital era, the cloud is *Útgarðr*:
1. **The Myth of the Cloud:** The "cloud" is simply someone else’s computer. When an individual stores their private journals, ritual invocations, family memories, and intellectual archives on cloud services, they forfeit sovereignty. Corporate terms of service can be altered arbitrarily, accounts can be terminated without appeal, and private thoughts are mined to train commercial neural networks.
2. **The Warding of Huginn's Well:** In Norse myth, Odin sends Huginn (thought) and Muninn (memory) out into the world, but they return to whisper directly into his ear. A sovereign mind must possess its own memory. Running local language models (via runtimes such as Ollama, llama.cpp, or local GPUs) ensures that your digital dialogue partner remains within your own hall. Your queries and reflections never leave your local machine.
3. **Plain-Text Durability:** Software formats come and go, but plain-text (such as Markdown) persists across decades. A personal knowledge repository built on plain text, linked data, and local folder structures remains accessible even during internet outages or societal disruptions, providing true ancestral longevity.
4. **The Sovereign Server as Digital Hearth:** Operating a home server (for local backups, media streaming, home automation, and network filtering) transforms computing hardware into a functional hearth. It provides warmth, utility, and self-reliance for the household without paying tribute to technocratic overlords.

## Key Principles

- **Local-First Architecture:** Always prefer applications that store data locally on your machine in open formats before considering cloud sync.
- **Warding the Digital Fence:** Implement network-level blocking (such as Pi-hole, firewalls, and VPNs) to keep telemetry, trackers, and uninvited commercial wights from penetrating your digital enclosure.
- **Offline Capability:** A sovereign system must remain fully functional when the internet connection is severed. True self-reliance includes offline documentation, local models, and local backups.
- **Private Thought as Sacred Sanctuary:** Your internal psychological and spiritual processing is sacred territory (*vé*). It must never be bartered away for convenience.
- **Hardware Stewardship:** Maintaining, upgrading, and repairing your own PCs, drives, and local servers preserves ancestral respect for physical matter and self-sufficiency.

## Distinctions

- **Sovereign Local Archive vs. Corporate Cloud Storage:** A local archive is owned, private, and durable; cloud storage is rented, surveilled, and subject to unilateral cancellation.
- **Technological Self-Reliance vs. Luddite Isolation:** Running local servers and models embraces advanced cutting-edge computation while rejecting external dependency and surveillance capitalism.
- **Intentional Knowledge Curation vs. Digital Hoarding:** Personal knowledge management requires active filtering, distillation, and synthesis, not just accumulating unread gigabytes of data.
