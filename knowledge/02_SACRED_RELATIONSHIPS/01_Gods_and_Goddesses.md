# Gods and Goddesses

## In Brief

The Holy Powers of the Northern world—the Aesir and the Vanir—are approached in the Heathen Third Path not as authoritarian despots demanding blind subservience, but as elder kin, teachers, guides, cosmic architects, and vital living realities honored through the reciprocal gifting cycle.

## Core Idea

Polytheism in the Heathen Third Path is a lived, experiential reality. The Northern pantheon is composed primarily of two divine tribes who fought a primal war, recognized the futility of mutual destruction, exchanged hostages, and merged into a unified sacred fellowship:
1. **The Aesir:** Primarily associated with cosmic order, social cohesion, sovereignty, wisdom, battle, law, and defense (such as Odin, Thor, Frigg, Tyr, and Heimdall).
2. **The Vanir:** Associated with primal nature, fertility, agriculture, wealth, sexual passion, magic, and deep earth-and-sea mysteries (such as Freyja, Freyr, and Njord).

In the Heathen Third Path, the relationship between humans and deities is characterized by mutual respect. There is no concept of original sin, and humans are not regarded as fallen, miserable worms who must grovel or beg for mercy. Instead, according to the Eddic creation myth (*Völuspá*), humanity was gifted life, breath (*önd*), inspired consciousness (*óðr*), and vital warmth (*lá*) by the three creator gods (Odin, Hœnir, and Lóðurr). Because we carry this divine spark in our physical bodies, we stand upright before the Holy Powers.

The Third Path balances two complementary modes of understanding the gods:
- **Hard/Substantive Polytheism:** The conviction that the deities are distinct, autonomous, living conscious entities with their own agency, wills, and cosmic domains.
- **Archetypal and Psychological Depth:** The recognition that the gods also embody deep universal patterns of human consciousness, cultural wisdom, and natural dynamics.

Rather than treating these perspectives as a contradictory either/or, the Third Path recognizes that the divine powers exceed narrow human intellectual definitions. Whether experienced in outward prayer or inward psychological reflection, our relationship with them is sustained through deeds, presence, and the gifting cycle.

## Key Principles

- **Elder Kin and Sacred Allies:** The gods are addressed as honored family members and trusted friends (*fulltrúi*), never as distant tyrants.
- **The Reciprocal Gifting Cycle:** "A gift demands a gift" (*gjöf sér til gjalda*). We offer libations of mead, beer, cider, water, food, or creative work; the gods return blessings of vitality, inspiration, good harvest, luck, and wisdom.
- **Pluralistic Devotion:** No practitioner is obligated to worship all deities equally. A person naturally forms closer bonds with deities aligned with their own wyrd, profession, nature, and life circumstances.
- **Honoring Both Tribes:** Reconciling the Aesir (order/consciousness) and Vanir (earth/wild nature) represents the foundational Heathen template for integrating civilization with ecology.

## Distinctions

- **Kinship vs. Subservience:** Monotheism demands absolute surrender to an all-powerful sovereign. Heathenry enters into an honorable covenant between sovereign beings.
- **Living Powers vs. Fairy Tale Allegories:** The gods are not comic book characters or literary fables; they are living forces of nature and spirit.
- **Pluralism vs. Monolatry:** Rejecting the claim that one single god is superior to all others, affirming the richness of a diverse, multifaceted cosmos.

In the 2011–2013 ritual texts, Volmarr provided step-by-step blót scripts invoking the Aesir and Vanir for solitary seasonal celebrations. In 2019, he introduced dedicated Vanatru and Asatru ritual variations, allowing practitioners to explore the specific flavor of each divine tribe.
